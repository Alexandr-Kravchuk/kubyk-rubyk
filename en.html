The last layer of the cube: what moves where

Keep the yellow center on top. In the top views, the bottom of the picture is the face toward you. Pieces are labeled so you can track where they move. The outer row of colors represents the centers of the side faces.

1. Cross2. Edges3. Flip edges4. Corners5. Twist corners

1. Yellow cross

Algorithm F (R U R' U') F'

Focus only on the four top-layer edges; ignore the corners for now (shown dark). A gray edge with a yellow strip on its side means its yellow sticker faces sideways.

What the algorithm does

Left edge (4) does not move at all.

Front (1) moves to the right and flips.

Right (2) moves to the back without flipping.

Back (3) moves to the front and flips.

A red dashed arrow means the edge flips (yellow on top becomes yellow on the side, and vice versa). A black arrow means it moves without flipping.

This is why you hold the cube this way: the front and back edges flip, so place the edges whose yellow stickers are not yet on top in those positions. Flipping them brings yellow to the top.

2. Put the edges in place

Algorithm R U R' U R U2 R' U

The cross is complete, so all edges have yellow on top. Now check whether each edge's side sticker matches the center below it. Each number identifies the edge's home: 1 — green, 2 — orange, 3 — blue, 4 — red.

What the algorithm does

Front and left edges swap places.

Right and back stay in place.

The yellow cross stays intact: all edges still have yellow on top.

The top-layer corners also move, which is fine — steps 4 and 5 will handle them.

If the correct edges are opposite each other, perform the algorithm once from any side to get two adjacent correct edges.

3. Flip the cross edges in place

Algorithm F (R U R' U') F' U — twice in a row

Use this when an edge is in the correct position but its yellow sticker faces sideways. Edges always flip in pairs: a single flipped edge is impossible on a normally assembled cube.

What the algorithm does

→

Back (3) and left (4) edges flip in place.

Front (1) and right (2) stay unchanged.

No edge changes position, so the results of the previous steps are preserved.

The top-layer corners move during this algorithm — steps 4 and 5 will fix them.

If the flipped edges are opposite each other, hold one on the left and perform the algorithm. The flipped edges will become adjacent. Rotate the whole cube so they are at the back and left, then repeat.

4. Put the corners in place

Algorithm U R U' L' U R' U' L

Now look at the four corners. A corner is in its correct position when its three colors match the centers of the three surrounding faces, even if yellow faces sideways. Letters identify each corner's home in the solved cube: A — front-right, B — back-right, C — back-left, D — front-left.

What the algorithm does

The corner at the front-right does not move.

The other three corners cycle along the arrows: back-left → front-left → back-right → back-left.

The edges do not move, so the results of the previous steps are preserved.

The corners may twist (leaving yellow on the side) — this is normal; step 5 fixes it.

If no corner is in the correct position, perform the algorithm once from any side to put one corner in place.

5. Twist the corners yellow-side up

Algorithm R' D' R D

What to do

1. Place an incorrectly oriented corner at the top-front-right and repeat the algorithm until this corner has yellow on top.

2. Turn only the top layer to bring the next incorrectly oriented corner to the same position. Repeat step 1.

3. Once all corners have yellow on top, turn the top layer to align the colors.

→
While you work on the corners, the bottom layers will look scrambled. This is normal: they will restore themselves at the end. Keep the whole cube in the same orientation; turn only the top layer between corners.
